Case study

NYC Taxi Intelligence

A real analytics build: Python ingestion, BigQuery, dbt models, quality tests, KPI marts, and a published dashboard.

Live Dashboard GitHub Repository
Question

Where should a fleet focus attention?

I treated the project like an operations problem: identify where taxi demand is strongest, where revenue is concentrated, which hours need coverage, and which high-volume routes show weaker efficiency.

Scope

April and May 2026 Yellow Taxi data

The modeled layer covers 7.59 million valid trips from official NYC TLC records, with KPI tables built for dashboard and Tableau-ready exports.

Modeling

Raw BigQuery tables are transformed into cleaned staging tables, reusable fact and dimension tables, and KPI marts for demand, revenue efficiency, operational pressure, and route analysis.

Data quality

The dbt test suite checks completeness, uniqueness, relationships, and business rules such as positive trip distance, positive revenue, valid duration, and KPI score ranges.

CI/CD

GitHub Actions checks the project on push and pull request by installing dependencies, compiling ingestion scripts, and validating the dbt project structure.

Insights

What the dashboard makes visible

Manhattan drives trip volume

Manhattan represents about 86.5% of modeled pickup trips, so coverage planning needs to keep that core market visible.

Airport zones matter for revenue

Queens has a smaller trip share, but airport zones such as JFK and LaGuardia contribute a larger revenue share.

Evening demand needs attention

The busiest pickup hours appear in late afternoon and evening, which supports schedule and availability planning.

Route alerts add operational detail

The route mart flags high-volume, lower-efficiency routes so the analysis does not stop at borough or zone totals.

Dashboard

Published operations dashboard

The dashboard brings together executive KPIs, fleet priority zones, borough portfolio views, capacity pressure hotspots, hourly demand, revenue efficiency, and route alerts.
